## 1. The three systems, independently

### A. ACE / ISA CPT — a coaching framework, not a training program
- **Objective:** safe, individualised, progressive programming for any client. **Source:** IFT model
  (Functional → Movement → Load/Speed stages, *Wk02 Ch2 p10–14*); screening and clearance (*Wk04 Ch5*);
  assessments (*Wk06 Ch10*); FITT-VP tables (*Wk07 Ch11 Table 11-10 p29–31*, Table 9-12 p16); periodization
  as macro → meso → micro with planned recovery (*Wk07 Ch11 p42–45*).
- **Strengths:** scope, screening, progression/regression logic, dose ranges by training status, recovery.
- **Limits:** gives ranges, not a plan. Has no peak-strength wave and no conditioning-format design. FITT-VP
  minimums are not a route to *extreme* strength (novice–intermediate resistance is 60–70% 1RM).
- **Physical qualities:** whatever the coach builds. **Use:** the outer frame and the gate for every client.

### B. Russian Strength Program V5 (Jon's 4-day adaptation)
- **Objective:** raise 1RM on squat, bench, deadlift, military press, weighted pull-up in **9 weeks**.
- **Structure (Source):** A = squat + bench (Mon/Thu); B = deadlift + press + pull-up (Tue/Fri). Mon/Tue are a
  fixed anchor (6×2 @ 80%); Thu/Fri progress 6×3 → 6×4 → 6×5 → 6×6 @ 80%, then 5×5 @ 85%, 4×4 @ 90%,
  3×3 @ 95%, 2×2 @ 100%, 1×1 @ 105% (retest). Each lift is trained 2×/week, 72 h apart. Accessories are fixed.
  No built-in deload; the anchor day is the light exposure. Auto-deload / RPE layer is in §4 of the reference.
- **Strengths:** clear specificity, %1RM loading, defined progression and retest; an eligibility gate (G1–G7).
- **Limits:** **no conditioning or aerobic component at all** (`russian-strength-program.md` §1); all four days
  are heavy strength; no power/carry/movement-variety design; short (9 wk); demanding on recovery.
- **Qualities developed:** maximal strength (weeks 5–9), hypertrophy/strength in weeks 1–4 (CPT mapping in §3a).
- **Use:** experienced, cleared lifter with a maximal-strength goal. Others go to §6 scaled entry.

### C. REVL (13-week block, captured 2025 Blk 3–4 and 2026 Blk 1–3)
- **Objective:** general work capacity, group adherence, a periodic maximal-strength test — not a single PB.
- **Structure:** Volume (3) → Build (3) → Deload (1) → Peak (3) → Rebuild (3).
  **Source (T1/T2):** Peak Wk 2 is coached 1RM/3RM testing (1RM barbell deadlift and push press/jerk, image-checked);
  Peak Wk 3 is conditioning baseline testing (studio-confirmed and file-named). Team-format rep totals are
  shared, not per person (T2).
  **T3 (programme as written, lower-bound counts):** load tokens climb from roughly 40–65% in Volume to 60–95% in
  Peak; density/team-format work is highest early and lowest in Peak; barbell hinge and squat appear in most
  sessions; every captured session is time-capped; carries and easy aerobic work are rare or absent.
- **Strengths:** block logic, built-in testing, work-capacity variety, adherence mechanics, power/Olympic and
  unilateral exposure.
- **Limits:** no individual screening or prescription; no easy aerobic work (T3, 0 of 623 sessions);
  time-capped only, so no untimed technique work; concurrent strength + conditioning in every session.
- **Use:** as the model for *how conditioning is periodised around strength*, and as the client's existing
  stimulus when they attend classes (`revl-class-integration.md`).

## 2. The gap analysis — what an "extremely strong, still functionally fit" client still needs

| Need | ACE/ISA | Russian | REVL | Gap → who fills it |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|---|
| Screening, clearance, individual regression | **Yes** | Gate only | No | Covered — ACE |
| Maximal-strength progression on the big lifts | Ranges only | **Yes** | Partial (Peak) | Covered — Russian |
| Retest / benchmark structure | Assessments | Wk 9 | **Yes** (Peak 2–3) | Covered — combine |
| Work capacity / mixed conditioning | Ranges | **No** | **Yes** | Covered — REVL |
| Power / ballistic exposure | Stage 3 | No | **Yes** | Covered — REVL |
| Unilateral lower, core/bracing | Yes | Accessories | Yes (T3) | Covered — keep light |
| **Easy aerobic base (Zone 1–2)** | Cardio FITT | **No** | **No** | **Gap — neither strength source; ACE gives dose ranges (*Wk05 Ch8*)** |
| **Carries / loaded holds** | No | No | Rare (T3) | **Gap — add small, cheap dose** |
| **Untimed technique / movement quality** | Yes | No | No (all capped) | **Gap — warm-up and primer blocks** |
| **Managing strength ↔ conditioning interference** | Recovery *Wk07 Ch11 p47–48* | Implicit | Not managed | **Gap — the integration rules in §5** |
| Fatigue budget across a week | Recovery | Auto-deload | Deload wk | **Gap — one explicit weekly budget (§5)** |
| Long-term structure past 9 weeks | Periodization principles | Ends at retest | 13-wk block | **Gap — coach-designed macrocycle (§7)** |

**The answer to the core question:** such a client still needs (1) an easy-aerobic base, (2) a small,
deliberate dose of carries and technique work, (3) an explicit interference and fatigue budget, and (4) a
macrocycle that continues past the 9-week wave. **Everything else already exists in one of the three sources**
— so the job is *subtraction and sequencing*, not adding more exercises.

## 3. What each source contributes to the integration (and what it must not)

- **ACE/ISA → the frame:** intake, G1–G7 gate, dose ranges, progression rules, scope limits.
  Must not be used as a reason to cap a screened, experienced lifter at 60–70% 1RM.
- **Russian → the strength spine:** %1RM wave, anchor + progression exposure per lift, 72 h spacing, retest.
  Must not be run on all 5 lifts *and* full conditioning; V5 allows a **subset of wave lifts** with the rest at
  a fixed maintenance dose (3 × 6–8 @ ~70%, `russian-strength-program.md` §3a).
- **REVL → the conditioning logic and the block shape:** volume/density high early and falling as strength
  intensity rises; a formal testing week; power, unilateral and mixed-modal variety.
  Must not be copied as a session list or day-emphasis map; nothing here is a claim about a client's own REVL week.

## 4. The design methodology (decision sequence)

Apply in order. Each step writes a line into the plan's evidence section.

1. **Gate and scope.** Intake; medical clearance (G1); G2–G7 each marked Pass/Fail/Unknown; Unknown = unmet.
   Unmet → scaled entry (§6 of the Russian reference), not this methodology.
2. **Goal ranking.** Name one *primary* quality (for this system: maximal strength) and at most one
   *secondary* (work capacity). "Both equally" is not a valid answer: primary gets a full dose, secondary the
   minimum effective dose (`coaching-decision-framework.md` §3, cited in the REVL file §10).
3. **Constraints.** Days/week, minutes/session, equipment, injuries, sleep/stress, other sport, whether the
   client already does REVL (→ branch B below).
4. **Choose the wave lifts.** Default up to 3 of the 5; the rest at maintenance. More lifts only if recovery
   (G6) supports it and conditioning dose is cut to match.
5. **Choose the split from the frequency need, not from a template.** Each wave lift needs 2 exposures/week
   about 72 h apart (Source). With 4 days: two paired sessions repeated (A/B/A/B) is the direct V5 layout.
6. **Assign conditioning by interference rules (§5)**, then size it against the fatigue budget.
7. **Add only the gap items (§2):** an easy-aerobic dose, a carry, a technique/primer block. Nothing else.
8. **Progression rules:** wave loads from the Source table; RPE/RIR hold-or-drop trigger and auto-deload from
   `russian-strength-program.md` §4; conditioning by *dose* (minutes/density), not by adding sessions.
9. **Testing:** 1RM only if G4 passes; otherwise estimated 1RM from a rep-max (Table 10-25). One repeatable
   conditioning baseline at start and end (REVL's Peak Wk 3 idea).
10. **Reassess** at the end of every block against the gate and the client's reported recovery.

**Branch B — client already trains at REVL.** Do not stack. Follow `revl-class-integration.md` §10 (Masters
2-day or replace REVL's strength-track days; nothing near Peak Wk 2–3; ask what the client actually does).

## 5. Interference and fatigue rules (Judgement, informed by Literature)

**Basis (Literature; searched 2026-09-20 — abstracts/summaries read, full papers not opened):**
- Wilson et al. 2012 (*JSCR* 26(8):2293–2307; 21 studies): interference depends on endurance modality,
  frequency and duration; **running, not cycling,** was associated with decrements in hypertrophy and strength.
- Schumann et al. (updated meta-analysis, *Sports Med*; 43 studies): concurrent training did **not** meaningfully
  reduce maximal strength or hypertrophy; **explosive strength** gains were attenuated, more so when strength and
  endurance were in the **same session**. *(Effect size quoted in a secondary summary, not checked against the paper.)*
- Eddens et al. 2018 (*Sports Med*, intra-session sequence): strength-then-endurance gave ~7% larger squat 1RM gains
  than the reverse; no order effect on hypertrophy or aerobic gains. *(Secondary summary.)*
- Petré et al. 2021 (*Sports Med*): reduced lower-body strength development in **trained** participants but not
  moderately trained or untrained; Huiberts et al. 2024 (59 studies): modest lower-body strength interference in
  males and power interference, no clear moderation by training status, and **no classified study of highly
  strength-trained people**. *(Both as reported in `research_notes/`; not opened by me.)*
- Schumann's same-session subgroup showed the explosive-strength attenuation; a subgroup with **≥ 3 h between modes** did
  not. That is a subgroup observation, not a universal 3-hour rule.
**Consequence for an experienced lifter:** the pooled "no loss of max strength" finding may not carry over to
someone who is already strong (Petré), and no study covers highly strength-trained people. So the template keeps
conditioning to one block, low-impact, after lifting, and monitors response. **Consequence:** the earlier "close to the worst-case interference pattern" wording in
`source/revl_vs_russian_program_comparison.md` §3.5 **overstates the risk to max strength** and should be softened;
the better-supported concern is **explosive strength and same-session, running-based work.**

Rules used in §8 (all Judgement):
- **Order and modality:** strength first (Eddens); conditioning after it; bike/rower/ski erg rather than running
  (Wilson). Power primers go *before* strength, fresh. Same-session conditioning has a known cost to explosive
  strength (Schumann) — accepted here because only 4 training days exist; if power is the secondary goal, move
  the hard block to its own session instead.
- **Near-maximal exposures:** at most 2 per rolling 72 h; none in the day before a progression session.
- **Conditioning volume falls as strength intensity rises** — mirrors REVL's own pattern (density/team work
  highest in Volume, lowest in Peak; T3).
- **Weekly budget:** ≤ 1 dedicated hard conditioning block, plus short finishers, plus easy aerobic sessions
  that do not count as training days. If freshness markers slip for ~1 week, remove the hard block first.
- **Fat-loss deficit running:** apply `russian-strength-program.md` §5 (maintenance in intensification weeks).

## 6. Periodisation — one macrocycle, not two calendars

**Coach-designed, 13 weeks (Judgement).** Deliberately borrows REVL's 13-week envelope and the Russian
9-week wave, but nests one inside the other:

| Weeks | Strength (Russian wave) | Conditioning (REVL-style logic) | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | V5 wk 1–4 (80%, volume rising) | Highest dose; density/mixed-modal | Accumulate; baseline test in wk 1 |
| 5–8 | V5 wk 5–8 (85 → 100%) | Falling to finishers + easy aerobic only | Intensify; protect the wave |
| 9 | V5 wk 9 (1×1 @105% → retest) | Nil except easy aerobic | 1RM (or estimated) retest |
| 10 | Deload / technique / reassess gate | Conditioning baseline **retest** | Recovery + benchmark |
| 11–13 | RPE-based rebuild, hypertrophy/volume, new wave lifts chosen | Rebuild dose, mixed-modal | Bridge to next block |

The wave is **Source** (V5); the 13-week nesting, week 10, and the Rebuild block are **Judgement/T4**.
Repeat with a different lift subset each cycle. Reassess G1–G7 at week 10.

## 8. Illustrative 4-day template

**Reference profile (illustrative, not a real client):** experienced, medically cleared lifter passing G1–G7;
barbell + rack + bike/rower; primary goal maximal strength on squat/bench/deadlift; secondary goal work
capacity; 60–75 min/session; 4 training days; not attending REVL classes. **Any real client must first complete
intake and the gate.**

**Wave lifts:** squat, bench, deadlift. **Maintenance (3 × 6–8 @ ~70%):** military press, weighted pull-up.
Loads = `round(pct × 1RM, 2.5 kg)`.

| Day | Session |
|---|---|
| **Mon — A anchor** | **Primer** 4×5 box jumps, full recovery (20 contacts). **Squat 6×2 @ 80%**, rest 3 min. **Bench 6×2 @ 80%**, 2–3 min. Bulgarian split squat 2–3 × 8–12/side @ RIR 2–3. Weighted pull-up 3×6–8 (maintenance). |
| **Tue — B anchor** | **Deadlift 6×2 @ 80%**, 3 min. Military press 3×6–8 (maintenance). Farmer carry 4×30 m. Face pulls 2–3 × 8–12. |
| **Wed** | Off. **Easy aerobic 30–40 min** (conversational, RPE 3–4). |
| **Thu — A progression** | Squat and bench per the wave table (wk 1: 6×3 @ 80% → wk 9: 1×1 @ 105%). Dips 2–3 × 8–12. **Primer:** 4×5 box jumps first (wk 1–6 only). |
| **Fri — B progression** | Deadlift per wave table. Weighted pull-up stays at maintenance (3 × 6–8 @ ~70%). RDL omitted (deadlift already loaded). **Work-capacity block (wk 1–4 only):** 12–16 min capped mixed-modal on bike/row/ski + light KB or DB work; not running. If the schedule allows, do it ≥ 3 h after lifting (see §5). |
| **Sat/Sun** | Off. One **easy aerobic 30–45 min** session. Mobility/walks. |

**Wave table (Source, V5; applied to squat, bench, deadlift):**
Wk 1 6×3 · Wk 2 6×4 · Wk 3 6×5 · Wk 4 6×6 (all @ 80%) · Wk 5 5×5 @ 85% · Wk 6 4×4 @ 90% ·
Wk 7 3×3 @ 95% · Wk 8 2×2 @ 100% · Wk 9 1×1 @ 105% (retest). Mon/Tue stay 6×2 @ 80% every week.

**Conditioning by phase (Judgement):** wk 1–4 the single Friday block (no finisher is appended to the other days — a finisher on every session is not a default); wk 5–8 a ≤ 6 min Friday finisher only;
wk 9 none; wk 10 baseline retest. Easy aerobic stays throughout.

**Rest:** 2–5 min on wave sets at ≥ 85% (Table 9-12, strength row); 3 min on the 80% anchors (Judgement — no table row covers 80% × 2–6); 60–90 s on accessories.
**Progression triggers:** RPE ≥ 9 on a progression day twice running → hold or drop ~5%; anchor day ≤ RPE 6 →
nudge the working 1RM up; failed 2-for-2 or drifting RPE → anchor-only week or −10%.
**Warm-up:** 5 min easy cardio, ankle/hip/thoracic mobility, ramp sets of the first lift.
**Recovery:** 7–9 h sleep target; easy aerobic on off days counts as recovery, not training; cut the hard
conditioning block first if freshness slips.

## 12. Research check of the template's load-bearing claims

I cannot check "all research" — the literature is far larger than any pass. What I did: list the claims the
template depends on, search for each, and grade how well each is supported. Summaries were read; **full papers
were not opened**, and the weakest items below have **no research check at all**.

| Claim in the template | Evidence found | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Strength before conditioning in the session | Eddens 2018: ~7% larger squat 1RM gains, no difference in hypertrophy or aerobic gains | Supported (meta-analysis; secondary summary) |
| Bike/row/ski instead of running | Wilson 2012: running, not cycling, linked to strength/hypertrophy decrements | Supported (meta-analysis; older) |
| Concurrent work does not wreck max strength | Schumann (43 studies): no meaningful loss of max strength or hypertrophy | Supported; **weakens** the earlier "worst-case interference" framing |
| Same-session conditioning may cost explosive strength | Schumann: explosive strength attenuated, more so same-session | Supported; **an accepted trade-off** here |
| Linear wave is a valid strength design | Harries 2015 (17 studies, 510 participants): no difference between linear and undulating for strength. Moesgaard 2022 (35 volume-equated studies; per `research_notes/`): periodized > non-periodized for 1RM (ES 0.31), undulating favoured over linear for strength, but the trained-subgroup estimate is uncertain. Evidence is **mixed** | Supported as a valid, planned-variation design; **the evidence neither proves linear best nor undulating clearly better for trained lifters** |
| Each lift 2×/week, 72 h apart | Grgic and Ralston 2018: when volume is equated, frequency has no significant effect on strength | **Weak as a science claim** — supported by the V5 source and ISA spacing, not by frequency research. Frequency is a volume-distribution choice |
| Box-jump primer builds power | de Villarreal 2009 (56 studies): plyometrics raise jump height and some max strength; best with > 15 sessions and > 40 jumps/session | The original 3×3 was **far below** that dose. Raised to 4×5 (20 contacts) and labelled a low-dose primer, not a full power block |
| Carries fill a gap | **No research checked.** Rests only on T3 REVL counts saying carries are rare | Unsupported by literature |
| Easy aerobic base (Zone 1) | ISA three-zone model (Source). No research checked on dose for strength athletes | Unsupported beyond ISA |
| Week 10 deload, auto-deload triggers, RPE hold-or-drop | **No research checked** | Expert practice only |

**Changes made to the template because of this review:** wk 1 progression corrected; accessory reps and rest
brought in line with the sources; primer dose raised and moved onto both squat days; interference rules rewritten
to match what the meta-analyses show; the frequency and periodisation claims downgraded.

## 13. Additional evidence from the Chalk research notes (other session; not re-opened by me)
- Ramos-Campo 2024 (14 studies): split vs full-body similar for strength and hypertrophy when volume is equated — so
  the choice of a 4-day split is a scheduling decision, not a proven optimum.
- Currier 2023 (network meta-analysis): higher loads rank best for strength, multiple sets for hypertrophy; most direct
  comparisons uncertain. Robinson 2024 / Grgic 2022 / Vieira 2022: strength gains are not tied to training to failure;
  hypertrophy tends to improve nearer failure; failure costs more acute fatigue. Pelland 2026: diminishing returns in
  volume, stronger for strength than hypertrophy.
- **Design rules taken from that note:** four days means four *total* planned days unless the client says otherwise
  (intake P19); record strength and conditioning experience separately; do not append a hard finisher to every session;
  keep the dose ledger explicit and leave unknown class content unknown.
