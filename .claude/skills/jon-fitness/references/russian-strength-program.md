# Russian Strength Program — the strength-block archetype

A **program archetype** for Mode B: a linear-periodization strength block based on the
**Russian Squat Routine** (the Soviet 6-week peaking cycle) and Jon's 4-day adaptation of it
(`Russian Strength Program V5`). Use it when a **screened, medically-cleared, Load/Speed-ready**
client's goal is maximal strength / hitting PBs on the barbell lifts — athlete or general
population.

This file follows the skill's labelling rule: **Course-supported** (the ISA notes state it,
cited) · **Applied from the course** (extrapolated from course principles) · **General
knowledge** (outside the notes — flagged, kept minimal).

---

## 1. What the archetype is

A single **mesocycle** (Course-supported: periodization = planned variation of volume &
intensity over a training cycle; macro → meso → micro; *Wk07 Ch11 p42–43*) run as **linear
periodization** — volume falls, intensity rises, week over week, ending in a strength retest
(Course-supported: *Wk07 Ch11 p44*; the course's own worked example, Table 10-20, ramps
microcycles 12 → 8 → 4 → … → 1 rep and finishes with a "goal assessment" interim week —
*Wk07 Ch11 p45*).

The barbell lifts on the wave are the **core / structural exercises** (multi-joint, axial-
loaded). Course-supported: *"typically only core exercises (major muscles, multi-joint) are
periodized, but all exercises can be varied for intensity and volume"* (*Wk07 Ch11 p42*).
Accessories stay at a fixed hypertrophy prescription and are **not** put on the wave.

### Where it sits in the IFT model

**Muscular Training — 3rd stage: Load / Speed Training.** Course-supported: the Load/Speed
stage is *"application of external loads … to create increased force production"* and its
methods *"include HIIT, plyometrics and power lifting"* (*Wk02 Ch2 p14*; also p10, Table 2-3).
Athlete branch: *"clients with goals for athletic performance will integrate exercises and
drills to build speed, agility, quickness, and power"* (*Wk02 Ch2 p10*).

The colloquial phrase "functional training" for heavy barbell work is **not** the course's
term — in the ISA/ACE model **Functional Training** is stage 1 (postural stability + kinetic-
chain mobility, bodyweight/core/balance; *Wk02 Ch2 p10–11*). Barbell strength work is
**Movement + Load/Speed**. Keep citations attached to the real stage.

## 2. Eligibility gate — check every item before offering the archetype

Record the result in the plan's **Strength Block — Eligibility Gate** section. Any unmet item
is **BLOCKING** for *this archetype* (not for the client's goal — route to §6 instead).

| # | Gate | Course basis | If unmet |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | **Medically cleared** per the ACSM algorithm; near-maximal lifting is vigorous intensity | *Wk04 Ch5 p8–9, p14*; scope *Wk01 Ch1 p7–8* | Record BLOCKING gap; do not prescribe ≥ moderate load/intensity beyond what the algorithm permits until cleared |
| G2 | **Experienced lifter** — the ≥80% 1RM entry load is a *FITT-VP "experienced"* value (novice→intermediate = 60–70%) | Resistance FITT-VP, *Wk07 Ch11 Table 11-10, p29–31* | Route to §6 scaled entry |
| G3 | **Movement competence** — passes the bend-and-lift/squat, push, pull, single-leg and rotation screens; no red-flag compensations | Movement screens, *Wk06 Ch10 p45–59* | Movement-phase block first (§6) |
| G4 | **No assessment contraindications for loaded testing** — 1-RM squat needs proper form + no low-back/knee pain; 1-RM bench needs proper form + no shoulder-problem history | *Wk06 Ch10 p69, p72* | Use estimated 1RM from a sub-maximal rep test (Table 10-25, *p75*); no true 1RM |

## 3. The three variants

Pick from the client's training age, recovery capacity, available days, and whether a fat-loss
deficit runs concurrently (§5).

### 3a. V5 — 9-week, 4-day (Jon's adaptation) — *default for an experienced lifter wanting a full peak*

- **Split:** Workout A (Mon & Thu) = Squat + Bench + accessories (Bulgarian split squat, dips).
  Workout B (Tue & Fri) = Deadlift + Military Press + Weighted Pull-up + accessories (RDL, face
  pulls). Each main lift trained 2×/wk, 72 h apart — Course-supported spacing (2–3 d/wk per
  muscle group, 48–72 h; *Wk07 Ch11 Table 11-10, p11*).
- **Wave** — Mon/Tue are a fixed anchor exposure every week; Thu/Fri carry the progression.
  Applied to **every wave lift** at the same %:

  | Week | Anchor (Mon/Tue) | Progression (Thu/Fri) | % 1RM (progression) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 6 × 2 | 80% |
  | 2 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 6 × 4 | 80% |
  | 3 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 6 × 5 | 80% |
  | 4 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 6 × 6 | 80% |
  | 5 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 5 × 5 | 85% |
  | 6 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 4 × 4 | 90% |
  | 7 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 3 × 3 | 95% |
  | 8 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 2 × 2 | 100% |
  | 9 | 6 × 2 @ 80% | 1 × 1 | 105% → **retest** |

- **CPT mapping of the loads:** weeks 1–4 (6×2–6×6 @ 80%) sit across **hypertrophy** (67–85%,
  6–12 reps) and the low edge of **strength**; weeks 5–8 (85–100%, ≤6 reps, 2–6 sets, 2–5 min
  rest) are **muscular strength**; week 9 singles are a **power / max-strength** expression and
  a **1-RM assessment** (Table 9-12, *Wk07 Ch11 p16*; assessment *Wk06 Ch10 p69–74*).
- **No separate deload** — the Mon/Tue 6×2 @ 80% is the built-in lighter exposure. Add an
  explicit low week if autoregulation triggers it (§4).
- **Wave-lift count:** default all 5. To run a subset (e.g. 3/5), the other lifts drop to a
  fixed **3 × 6–8 @ ~70%** "maintenance" prescription for the block — Applied from the course
  (hypertrophy/strength maintenance dose; minimum-dose logic *Wk07 Ch11 p47*).

### 3b. Classic — 6-week, 3-day — *one focus lift, meet-style peak*

The original Russian Squat Routine. One lift on the wave (usually the squat); 3 sessions/wk
alternating a fixed **6 × 2 @ 80%** day with the progression day. Weeks 1–3 accumulate volume
(6×3 → 6×4 → 6×5 → 6×6 @ 80%), weeks 4–6 raise intensity (5×5 @ 85%, 4×4 @ 90%, 3×3 @ 95%,
2×2 @ 100%, 1×1 @ 105–110%), retest at the end of week 6. Everything else is maintained at
3 × 6–8. **General knowledge:** published expectation is +5–10% on the trained 1-RM; written
for experienced lifters with a solid base who are peaking, and demanding on recovery.

### 3c. Masters — 8-week, 2-day — *reduced recovery capacity / time-limited / bridging up*

2 sessions/wk. Same volume→intensity shape stretched over 8 weeks, with the anchor day run at
**60–70%** rather than 80% and a gentler top end (stops around 2×2 @ 95–100%). Retest optional;
for general-population clients use an **estimated 1RM from a rep-max**, not a true single.
**General knowledge:** this mirrors the commonly-published "masters" adaptation of the routine.

## 4. Modernisation / autoregulation layer

Keeps the block flexible without leaving what the course supports.

- **Target load = `round(pct × 1RM, 2.5 kg)`** — the same arithmetic as the source spreadsheet.
  `scripts/russian_block.py` generates the whole block from entered 1RMs (or from an RPE-
  anchored top set → estimated 1RM). `--taper-weeks N` appends N light taper microcycles;
  `--meet` appends a 3-attempt meet/retest week and drops the wave's own in-block 1×1 test.
- **RPE / RIR check (General knowledge, aligned with the course's use of RPE as an intensity
  metric — *Wk05 Ch8 p15–20* — and its double-progression / 2-for-2 logic — *Wk07 Ch11 p7,
  p20*):** if the progression-day load hits RPE ≥ 9 (≈ 0–1 reps in reserve) two sessions
  running, hold the load or drop ~5%; if an anchor day sits ≤ RPE 6, nudge the working 1RM up.
- **Estimated-1RM refresh:** update the driving 1RM from rep-max data mid-block rather than
  forcing true maxes (sub-maximal strength assessment, Table 10-25, *Wk06 Ch10 p75*).
- **Auto-deload:** insert a low microcycle (anchor-only week, or −10% loads) when 2-for-2 fails
  across a week or RPE drifts up on the anchor day — Course-supported rationale: periodization's
  main benefit is planned physical & mental recovery (*Wk07 Ch11 p42*).
- **Tempo:** controlled; 1–3 s concentric / 2–4 s eccentric, ~6 s reps while a new lifter is
  still grooving a pattern (*Wk07 Ch11 p17*).
- **Bar-velocity / velocity-loss cutoffs:** General knowledge, not in the notes — offer only if
  asked and label it.

## 5. Fat-loss integration

Many clients want this block *and* fat loss. Handle it in-scope and honestly.

**Scope (unchanged):** general, non-medical nutrition information only — **no meal plans, no
macro prescriptions, no medical nutrition therapy**. Refer to a **registered dietitian** for
anything detailed or for disordered-eating signs (*Wk01 Ch1 p7–8*; *Wk09 Ch6 p5, p22*).

**What the course says about fat loss (Course-supported, *Wk09 Ch12 p29–33*):**
- Behavioural weight loss = **caloric deficit 500–1000 kcal/day → 1–2 lb/week**, split roughly
  **−250 kcal intake / +250 kcal expenditure**, plus behavioural strategies. Refer to an RD for
  the diet.
- Resistance: 2–3 d/wk, 8–12 reps × 2–4 sets, full body; adequate **protein to preserve lean
  tissue**.
- Maintaining current weight can itself count as progress.

**On cardio volume — read the population.** Ch 12's weight-management cardio target (**≥5 d/wk,
< VT1 / RPE 3–4, 30–60 min/day → 150–250 min/week, low-impact**; *Wk09 Ch12 p32*) is written
for the **clients-with-obesity** population, where the priority is maximising energy expenditure
and there is no competing strength peak. **Do not treat 150–250 min/week as a fixed target for
a lean, trained client on a strength block** — for them, cardio is dosed to *energy balance and
recovery headroom*: enough Zone 1–2 work to support the chosen deficit, capped by what still
leaves the wave lifts recovering. That is often **well below** the Ch 12 band, especially in the
intensification/peak weeks. Applying the obesity figure to an athletic client is extrapolation —
label it, and let recovery, not a minute count, set the ceiling *(Wk07 Ch11 p47–48)*.

**The training levers the trainer fully controls (Applied from the course):**
- **Conditioning volume** dosed to the deficit and to recovery (see above) — place Zone 1–2
  work on upper-body or off days, and keep hard intervals away from heavy lower-body sessions so
  squat/deadlift recovery is protected (session components & recovery — *Wk07 Ch11 p18, p47–48*).
- **Accessory density** — supersets / compound sets / circuits raise energy cost without adding
  wave stress (*Wk07 Ch11 p13*).
- **NEAT / daily steps** — a low-cost expenditure lever that doesn't tax recovery; ≥7000
  steps/day, add ≥2000/day toward a target (*Wk05 Ch8 p10, p26*). Prefer raising steps over
  adding structured cardio when recovery is tight.

**The recovery conflict — a hard peak in a deficit degrades recovery and the retest.** Choose
one, and record it in the plan's **Deficit Periodisation** section:
1. **Deficit within the block** — moderate deficit (≈ −250 to −500 kcal/day, the client's or
   an RD's call) during the accumulation weeks; **maintenance** during the intensification /
   peak weeks (V5 wk 6–9; Classic wk 4–6).
2. **Sequential blocks** — a dedicated fat-loss block first (higher conditioning, denser
   accessories, larger deficit), then eat at maintenance and run the strength block for the
   peak.

**"Lose fat quickly / more aggressive" (client asked for this — General knowledge beyond the
course):** a faster rate needs a larger energy deficit, which is a **nutrition decision for the
client and their RD**, not a training prescription. Present it as an option with the trade-offs
stated plainly — greater loss of lean mass and strength, blunted performance on the wave,
higher fatigue, lower adherence — and note that anything past the course's 500–1000 kcal/day
band is outside ISA guidance. On the training side the trainer can support a faster push with
more conditioning and circuit-style accessories; the wave itself should usually be run on a
smaller subset of focus lifts (§3a) or the Masters variant when the deficit is aggressive.

## 6. When NOT to use it — the scaled entry

For a general-population client with little training background (fails G2/G3), don't refuse the
goal — build the on-ramp:

1. **Movement-phase block** — 5 primary movement patterns, light load (~50–60% 1RM or
   unloaded), reps over intensity, coach technique (Tell-Show-Do, *Wk02 Ch4 p13–18*); *Wk02
   Ch2 p12–13; Wk07 Ch11 p21–28*.
2. **Foundational Load/Speed block** — 60–70% 1RM, 8–12 reps, 2–4 sets, double progression /
   2-for-2, ~5% increments; *Wk07 Ch11 Table 11-10 p29–31, p7, p20*. 8–12 weeks.
3. **Re-assess the gate** (§2). When G2–G4 pass, start the **Masters variant** (§3c) as the
   bridge onto the Russian structure, then V5/Classic in a later block.

Document the client's **starting numbers** (rep-maxes → estimated 1RM, Table 10-25) at the
outset so end-of-block progress is measurable without a true 1-RM test.

## 7. Retest protocol

Set the method in the plan's **Retest Plan** section, chosen by client type:

- **Athlete / experienced (G4 passes):** true **1-RM or 3-RM** test on the primary lift(s) in
  the final week. Follow the course: proper form prerequisite, no contraindicating pain/history,
  **spotting** with a stable base and neutral spine, rack and safeties set (*Wk06 Ch10 p69,
  p72, p74*). Pre-test rules: 6–8 h sleep, no vigorous exercise 24 h prior, hydrated (*Wk04
  Ch8 p70*). Stop-test criteria apply (*Wk04 Ch8 p71*). Time it to the competition / testing
  date for a competitive athlete — the sport calendar drives the macrocycle. For a dated meet,
  generate with `--meet --taper-weeks 1` (or 2): the wave stops one week early, a light taper
  microcycle is added, then a 3-attempt (opener ~92% / second ~100% / third ~105%) meet day.
  The taper %s and attempt selection are **General knowledge** layered on the course skeleton —
  label them, and adjust attempts live off how the openers move.
- **General population:** **no true 1-RM.** Retest with the **same rep-max** used at baseline
  (e.g. 5RM), convert to estimated 1RM (Table 10-25), and report the **start → end delta** and
  the loads carried each week. This is the course's sub-maximal strength assessment, *"appropriate
  for inexperienced exercisers"* (*Wk06 Ch10 p75*).

## 8. Building the plan (Mode B additions)

In `B4 — Design the program`, after step 1 (Program strategy):

1. Goal is maximal strength / PBs on the barbell lifts? → consider this archetype.
2. Run the **§2 eligibility gate**. Any unmet item → §6 scaled entry instead; stop here.
3. **Branch:** competitive athlete (sport, position, season phase → macrocycle timing; add
   SAQ/plyometrics/power per *Wk07 Ch11 p33–41*, Table 11-12/11-13, respecting plyo
   contraindications) vs general population (physique / PBs / fat loss).
4. Pick the **variant** (§3) and the **wave lifts** (default 5; subset → others at 3×6–8 @ ~70%).
5. If fat loss is concurrent → **§5**: pick deficit-within-block or sequential; set conditioning
   and accessory density; keep nutrition to general information + RD referral.
6. Set **autoregulation rules** (§4) and the **retest method** (§7).
7. Write the Markdown using `templates/strength_block_plan.md` sections in addition to the
   standard `client_fitness_plan.md` structure. Generate the CSV with
   `scripts/russian_block.py` (see `templates/strength_block.csv` for the shape) — pass
   `--fat-loss` for the concurrent-deficit case, `--meet --taper-weeks N` for a dated meet.
   The script already emits warm-up, cool-down and conditioning rows; review and adjust them.
8. Validate (B7) — `validate_plan.py` runs extra checks when it sees a strength block (gate
   recorded, retest date present, accessories not on the wave, deficit + peak not both
   aggressive).

## 9. Traceability example
