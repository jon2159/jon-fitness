# Chalk Performance Training — bounded design reference

**Scope.** Chalk is a *supplemental program-design and delivery reference*. It is **not** one of the three
foundations (ISA CPT, Russian Strength Program, REVL) and never overrides screening, the Russian eligibility gate,
REVL provenance, or a client's constraints. Public pages establish what Chalk *advertises*; they do not establish
that any Chalk program works better than the foundations. **Do not reproduce a Chalk program, and never present
one of our integrated designs as "Chalk's prescription."**

## 2. Claims that must NOT be used as evidence
- "Up to threefold muscle growth from more frequent training" (FBA page): no identifiable study; marketing.
- "Part of U.S. Military fitness protocols" (landing page): unsupported by any cited source here.
- Any single "Chalk template" assembled from several tracks, any universal duration, split or deload calendar.
- Testimonials, membership numbers, coach credentials — none demonstrates efficacy.

## 3. What the skill may borrow (as coach judgement, labelled as such)
1. **Order qualities by their quality-sensitivity** — explosive/technical work first while fresh, priority strength
   next, then goal-relevant accessories, then conditioning (seen in the PowerJacked and Powerbuilding samples; also
   consistent with Eddens 2018 for strength-before-endurance and Schumann 2022 for explosive strength).
2. **Stable measurable anchors with purposeful variation** around them, so progress stays assessable.
3. **Executable delivery:** substitutions, load history, a session time budget. These improve adherence and plan
   quality; they do not replace coaching or screening.
4. **Track choice by goal:** different goals warrant different structures — full-body, upper/lower and
   power-emphasis tracks all appear in Chalk's own catalogue, so no single split is mandatory.

## 4. What Chalk does NOT supply here
Weekly muscle-group volumes, progression percentages, intensity distribution, recovery algorithms, testing, or
conditioning distribution for any track. Treat all of these as **unknown**; fill them from ISA, Russian or REVL
material or from labelled coaching judgement, never with a plausible-sounding Chalk detail.

## 5. Use in a plan
Record any borrowed idea in the plan's evidence section as: *"Chalk-informed session ordering (public sample,
retrieved 2026-09-20) — coach judgement"*, with the client fact and the decision it supported. If a decision needs
paid or unpublished Chalk detail, mark it unknown and proceed without it.

## 6. Evidence for borrowing (papers searched 2026-09-20; abstracts/summaries read, full papers not opened)

| Chalk feature | Research | What it supports | Use in the skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daily/high-frequency distribution of volume (FBA Vol.1) | Schoenfeld 2016 (*Sports Med*): ≥ 2×/wk per muscle beat 1×; Schoenfeld 2019 volume-equated meta-analysis and Grgic/Ralston 2018: no frequency effect when volume equal; Pelland 2026 (per `research_notes/`) | Frequency is a **way to distribute volume**, not a growth multiplier. The "threefold growth" claim has no support | Choose frequency for feasibility and recovery; never claim a frequency advantage |
| High-rep accessories (3×15–20 laterals, curls, calves) beside heavy compounds (5×2 deadlift) | Schoenfeld 2017 and Lopez 2021 (network meta-analysis): heavy loads favour 1RM strength; hypertrophy is similar across loads when sets are taken near failure | Heavy anchors for strength + lighter accessories for size is defensible | Matches V5 (heavy wave + 8–12 accessories); accessories need effort near failure to count |
| Power/jump first, then squat (PowerJacked sample) | Seitz & Haff 2016 (*Sports Med*): potentiation depends on strength level and rest (~3–7 min quoted); de Villarreal 2009: plyometrics raise jump height | Explosive work is best done fresh. **A box-jump primer is not a potentiation protocol** — the ordering rationale here is quality, not PAP | Keep power before strength; do not claim potentiation |
| Exercise variation ("hidden progressive overload") | Fonseca 2014 (untrained, one exercise vs varied): favoured variation; Baz-Valle 2019 (resistance-trained men): similar strength responses; a 2022 *JSCR* systematic review exists (not read) | Evidence is **mixed**; variation may help motivation but is not shown superior in trained lifters | Keep priority lifts fixed so progress is measurable; vary accessories for adherence |
| Recovery week every 5th week (Vol.1) | Bell et al. 2023 (Delphi consensus); Coleman et al. 2024 (RCT in **resistance-trained** adults, abstract read): a full week off did not help, and lower-body strength improved more with continuous training | Deloads are **expert practice, not RCT-proven**; a full break isn't shown to add gains | Prefer autoregulated reductions (RPE trigger) over a fixed rest week; keep a planned test/reassess week |
| Mixed-modal AMRAP (S&C sample) | HIFT meta-analysis (19 studies, 911 adults): improves strength and fitness; Feito 2018: ~30% of surveyed CrossFit participants reported an injury in 12 months | Works, but injury risk is real and is linked to technique, intensity and coaching | Low-skill, capped, controlled; never after maximal technical lifts |
| Sprint/interval work (PowerJacked) | Ferraro-Farro 2026 (abstract read; 9 RCTs, 177 healthy adults): no significant strength difference vs resistance training alone, higher VO2max. **Sabag 2018 (abstract read): concurrent HIIT lowered lower-body strength gains (ES −0.248); cycling HIIT trended worse than running (ns); longer inter-modal rest may reduce it** | Interval work may be the **most strength-compatible** conditioning form | Preferred conditioning form for a strength-primary client (bike, not running) |
| Load history, timers, alternatives (app) | Michie et al. 2009 (*Health Psychol*, 122 evaluations): self-monitoring plus ≥ 1 other control-theory technique gave larger effects (0.42 vs 0.26) | Tracking helps behaviour change generally (physical activity and healthy eating), not resistance training specifically | Require a training log and weekly review in every plan |

**Grades:** frequency, load and concurrent-training rows are supported by meta-analyses; variation and deload are
mixed or expert-practice; power ordering and the recovery week are Judgement with partial support; app delivery
is supported only indirectly (behaviour-change evidence, not strength outcomes).
