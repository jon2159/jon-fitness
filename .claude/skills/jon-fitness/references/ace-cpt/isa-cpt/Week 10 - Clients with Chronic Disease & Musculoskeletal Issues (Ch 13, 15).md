DIABETES – SAFETY
- Check blood sugar before, right after, and in the hours following an exercise
- Check blood sugar every 30 to 60 minutes during prolonged workout
- Start the exercise with blood glucose ≥100 mg/dL
(5.6 mmol/L) and ≤250 mg/dL (13.9 mmol/L) (unless no ketosis detected)
- If blood glucose is below 100 mg/dL, administer 15-
20 g of fast-acting CHO

## Page 51

ASTHMA – EIS
- Exercise-Induced Bronchoconstriction (EIS)
- An asthmatic attack following moderate to vigorous exercise
- Severity of EIS is related to the intensity of exercise
- Symptoms usually peak 5-10 min after exercise and last for 20-30 min
- Some might develop hacking cough 2-12 h after exercise that can last for 1-2 d i ia d a
- ~50% of EIB leads to a relative refractory period (up to 2 h)
- ~50% of EIB leads to mild asthmatic response 6-8 h after the initial bronchoconstriction
- Strategy to reduce EIS
- Breathe in through nose to humidify and warm up the air

## Page 52

ASTHMA – EXERCISE GUIDELINES
- Client to review the use of medications with their physician
- Have rescue medication at all times
- Avoid asthma triggers; consider moving indoors on extreme hot or cold days or when pollen counts/air pollution are high. Consider wearing a face mask when exercising in cold
- Plenty of hydration before, during and after exercise
- Prolonged warm-up: 10-15 min vigorous/interval; prolonged cool-down
- Start low and increase intensity slowly, contingent on client’s condition
- Closely observe for early signs of EIB
- If an asthma attack is not relieved by medication, activate emergency medical system
- Asthmatics often respond best to exercise in mid-to-late morning
