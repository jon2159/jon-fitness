EFFECT OF MEDICATIONS ON
HEART RATE RESPONSE
Anti-hypertensives primarily affect one of four different sites:
- Heart: to reduce its force of contraction.
- Peripheral blood vessels: to open or dilate them to allow more room for the blood.
- Brain: to reduce the sympathetic nerve outflow.
- Kidneys: to reduce blood volume by excreting more fluid.
The most common antihypertensives:
- Beta blockers.
- Calcium channel blockers.
- Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitors.
- Angiotensin-II receptor antagonists.
- Diuretics.

Factors Affecting Heart Rate Assessment
- Smoking and tobacco products (↑ resting HR; ↑ or -- exercise HR)
- Caffeine (↑ or resting and exercise HR)
- Environmental temperatures (hot temperatures: ↑ RHR and exercise HR in; cold temperatures: variable)
- Altitude (↑ HR at altitudes greater than approximately 4,000 feet or 1,200m)
- Stress (↑ resting and exercise HR)
- Body position (↓ HR when supine, ↑ HR from supine to seated position or standing position)
- Time of day (↓ HR in the morning, ↑ or -- during afternoon or evening)
- Medications (variable)
Note: ↑ = increase, ↓= decrease, -- = no significant change

FITNESS ASSESSMENTS -
TEST TERMINATION
Indications for Terminating Exercise Testing
- Onset of angina or angina-like symptoms
- Drop in systolic BP of >10 mmHg from baseline BP despite an increase in workload
- Excessive rise in BP: systolic pressure >250mmHg or diastolic pressure >115mmHg
- Shortness of breath, wheezing, leg cramps, or claudication
- Signs of poor perfusion (e.g. ataxia, dizziness, pallor, cyanosis, cold or clammy skin, or nausea)
- Failure of HR to rise with increased exercise intensity
- Noticeable change in heart rhythm
- Client’s request to stop
- Physical or verbal manifestations of sever fatigue
- Failure of testing equipment

WALKING TEST
- Developed to estimate VO2max for men and women ages 18-69 years.

1.5 MILE (2.4 KM) RUN
- Measure the time it takes for client to run 1.5 mile
- Instruct clients to cover the 1.5 mile (2.4 km) distance in the fastest possible time.
Walking is allowed, but the objective is to complete the distance in as a short amount of time as possible
- Record the time to finish
Use the equation to estimate the client’s VO2max (ml/lg/min): VO2max (ml/kg/min) = 88.02 – (0.1656 x BW [kg]) – (2.76 x time [nearest hundred of a min] + (3.716 x gender*).
