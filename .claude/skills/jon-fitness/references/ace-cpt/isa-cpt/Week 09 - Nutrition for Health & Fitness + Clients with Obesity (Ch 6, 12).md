FUELING BEFORE, DURING,
AND AFTER EXERCISE
Pre-Exercise Fueling
Carb loading – eat a meal 4-6 hours prior to the workout
Eating a small carb-and-protein snack 30-60mins before exercise increases glucose availability
Pre-exercise snack should be high in carbs; low in fat and fiber, moderate in protein;
approximately 400-800 calories
Fueling during Exercise
Athletes should consume glucose-containing snacks and consume 30-60g of carbs/hour of training
Consume in small amounts in 15-20min intervals
Adding protein to carbs during exercise helps improve performance
Post-Exercise Replenishment
Post-workout meals include mostly carbs with some protein
Carb intake of 1.5g/kg of body weight in the first 30mins after exercise, then every 2 hours for 4-6 hours
Refueling should begin within 30mins after exercise and followed by high-carb meal within 2 hours

EATING DISORDERS
Anorexia Nervosa
An eating disorder characterized by refusal to maintain body weight
Intense fear of gaining weight or becoming fat
May practice excessive exercise, use laxatives and vomit to reduce weight (but to a lesser extent than Bulimia)
Body image disturbances
Bulimia Nervosa
An eating disorder characterized by recurrent episodes of uncontrolled binge eating and behavior such as self-induced vomiting
May be repeated several times a week or even several times a day
Binge Eating Disorder
An eating disorder characterized by by frequent binge eating (without purging)
Feelings of being out of control when eating body dysmorphia media depression low self esteem anxiety mentalillness peer pressure perfectionism guilt picky eating

EXERCISE AND WEIGHT
MANAGEMENT

A client-centered approach to working with clients with obesity
❖ Lifestyle interventions and increased physical activity should be the first options
❖ Utilize 3 components of behavioral weight loss program:
Caloric reduction to produce a deficit of 500-1000kcal daily
1. Increased physical activity
2. Behavioral-change strategies
3. ❖ Refer obese clients to RDs for a more personalized dietary plan
❖ Combination of these factors allows for better weight-management success
