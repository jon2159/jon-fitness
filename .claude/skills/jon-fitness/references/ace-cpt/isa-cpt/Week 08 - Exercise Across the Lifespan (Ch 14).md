## Page 7

PROGRAM CONSIDERATIONS -
SAFETY
Risk factors for children:
- Epiphyseal plate (growth plate) not fully fused
Guidelines:
- Ensure proper supervision
- Emphasize proper technique/forms at all times
- FW/BW exercises (traditional machine might not be suitable)
- NO 1RM lifts, no sudden explosive moves, or compete with other children while performing muscular training
- Teach proper breathing during exercise
- Encourage hydration
- Encourage communication with PT, coach, parent, or teacher
- Variety, fun, periodization to avoid overuse injury

## Page 8

PROGRAM CONSIDERATIONS -
ENVIRONMENT
Greater risks compared to adults
- In the Heat:
- Higher surface area to mass ratio
- Lower exercise economy
- Diminished sweating capacity
- Lower cardiac output at a similar workload
- In the Cold:
- Dehydration
- Hypothermia
- Frostbite
- Dry air exacerbates asthma or exercise-induced bronchoconstriction

EXERCISE GUIDELINES -
PREGNANCY
- Previously active women with no health complication may continue their previous level of exercise as tolerated
- Previously inactive women should begin low and slowly progress to moderate intensity exercise
- Use talk test and stay below VT1
- Focus on hydration and balancing caloric intake; some may take a small snack prior to exercise to avoid hypoglycemia
Avoid:
- Activities that risk falling
- Contact sports
- Extreme heat
- Prolonged supine position after about 20 weeks of gestation
(risk of hypotension, ie., low blood pressure)

- Calf pain or swelling

Balance exercises and training progressions for older adults
Position
Balance exercise
Sitting
-Sit upright and complete progressions listed below.
-Perform leg activities (heel, toe, or single-leg raises, marching).
Standing
-“Clock” – balance on one leg (other leg at 45° or 90°), personal trainer calls out time, client moves nonsupport leg to the time called out, alternate legs.
-Performing leg activities (heel, toe, or single-leg raises - 45° or 90° angle, marching).
-Spelling – balance on one leg, personal trainer asks the client to spell word working with nonsupport leg (i.e., client’s name, day of week, favorite food), alternate leg.
In-motion
-Heel-to-toe walking along 15-ft line on floor (first with and then without partner.
-“Excursion” – alternating legs, lunge over a space separated by two lines of tape. Progress to hopping or jumping (using single-leg or double-leg) back and forth across the space.
-Dribble basketball around cones that require the client to change direction multiple times.
Training
-Arm progression: use surface for support, hands on thigh, hands folded across chest.
progression
-Surface progressions: chair, balance disc, foam pad, physioball.
-Visual progressions: open eyes, sun glasses or dim room lighting, closed eyes.
-Task progression: single tasking, multi-tasking (i.e., balance exercise + pass/catch ball)
